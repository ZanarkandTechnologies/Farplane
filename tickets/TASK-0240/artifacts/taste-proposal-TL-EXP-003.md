# TL-EXP-003 Taste Proposal

## Context

TL-EXP-002 was rejected because the Telegram request felt like an internal
planning option sheet. TL-EXP-003 uses the updated TasteProposal contract and
asks Kenji to react like a first buyer: does this make Pocket Intern feel
desirable enough to draft the social thread?

```text
TasteProposal:
  task_context: >
    We are making the first AGI Toy Shop social thread for Pocket Intern, a
    tiny wind-up office assistant that stamps "ship it" on sticky notes and
    comes with a fake employee badge. The task is not to publish yet; it is to
    choose the customer-facing marketing premise worth drafting.
  bigger_problem: >
    People do not need another productivity framework. They need a funny,
    physical way to end the endless almost-shipping loop: rewrite the post,
    rename the project, tweak the launch note, and never get the tiny win out
    the door.
  proposed_solution: >
    Position Pocket Intern as the fake coworker that makes shipping physical.
    You write the thing on a sticky note, wind up the intern, stamp "ship it,"
    and turn a vague intention into a desk ritual you can photograph, send to a
    friend, or use as a tiny public commitment.
  customer_pitch: >
    Imagine the fake intern who makes shipping physical. It cannot write your
    launch plan, join Slack, or fix your life. It can do one perfect thing:
    stamp "ship it" on the note you have been avoiding until your brain accepts
    the bit and sends the thing.
  title: Imagine The Fake Intern Who Makes Shipping Physical
  one_line_bet: >
    The best social thread sells Pocket Intern as a charming desk ritual: a
    toy employee that turns hesitation into a stamped, shareable shipping
    moment.
  audience_or_buyer: >
    Founders, builders, students, creators, and gift buyers who like weird
    internet objects that are funny first and weirdly useful second.
  taste_insight: >
    The product should not be pitched as novelty merch. The magic is that a
    silly physical object can lower the stakes of shipping. The stamp turns
    "I should finish this someday" into "I stamped it, so now I have to send
    it."
  artifact_shape: >
    8-post X/Twitter thread that reads like a mini product myth: the fake
    intern arrives, reveals the shipping ritual, proves it with concrete use
    cases, and lands as an impulse gift.
  core_angle: >
    Your smallest coworker has one job: make the moment of shipping feel real.
  execution_beats:
    - beat_1: >
        Open with a vivid product promise: "Imagine the fake intern who makes
        shipping physical."
    - beat_2: >
        Name the customer pain: everything is almost ready, but "almost" has
        no satisfying finish line.
    - beat_3: >
        Introduce Pocket Intern as a tiny wind-up employee with a badge and
        one sacred workplace power: the "ship it" stamp.
    - beat_4: >
        Explain the ritual: write one scary or stuck task on a sticky note,
        wind the intern, stamp it, and ship before the bit wears off.
    - beat_5: >
        Show buyer-ready scenes: launch the tiny feature, send the awkward
        email, submit the class project, text a stamped-note photo to a friend.
    - beat_6: >
        Make the product emotional: the toy does not make you productive; it
        makes finishing feel less lonely and more like a game.
    - beat_7: >
        Turn it into a gift: for the friend with 47 ideas, the founder with
        too many tabs, or the student who needs a ridiculous desk boss.
    - beat_8: >
        Close with the commercial line: "Pocket Intern. Under $40. Zero equity.
        One job: stamp the thing into the world."
  why_it_could_win:
    - reason_1: >
        It sells the product through a concrete behavior, not an abstract hook.
    - reason_2: >
        It makes the badge and stamp feel like props in a ritual people can
        imagine using or gifting.
    - reason_3: >
        It keeps the absurdity but gives it a real emotional job: permission to
        finish.
  what_would_make_it_cringe:
    - risk_1: >
        If the copy gets too self-help or founder-productivity coded, the joke
        will flatten.
    - risk_2: >
        If the thread explains the ritual too clinically, it will feel like a
        plan instead of a toy someone wants.
  references_or_taste_pack: >
    AGI Toy Shop scenario; Pocket Intern badge + "ship it" stamp; Kenji's
    feedback to make the request feel like marketing; weird and useful desk
    object energy.
  feedback_question: >
    Does this customer-facing premise feel strong enough to draft the social
    thread? Reply approve, revise, or reject with one reason.
  next_if_approved: >
    Freeze this best-bet premise and draft the 8-post social thread. Do not
    publish externally.
```
