## ConceptCard A: Your First Hire Should Be Plastic

```text
title: Your First Hire Should Be Plastic
one_line_bet: Make Pocket Intern feel like the cheapest, funniest teammate a founder can put on their desk.
why_it_might_impress: It turns the toy into a founder joke with a buying reason: morale, desk ritual, and "ship it" theater.
taste_priors:
  - Founder Twitter likes jokes that smuggle a real truth.
  - The badge and stamp are concrete props, so the thread can stay visual even without images.
  - Under-$40 impulse gift works when the buyer instantly knows who they would send it to.
hook: "Your first hire should be plastic. It never asks for equity, always says 'ship it,' and fits next to your keyboard."
execution_shape: 7 tweets: hook; the loneliness of building; introduce Pocket Intern; three toy-as-office-bit examples; why physical rituals matter; gift CTA.
risk: Could read too startup-insider if the thread overplays fundraising jokes.
feedback_question: Keep, revise, or reject the hook and argument?
```

## ConceptCard B: The Intern Who Only Approves Shipping

```text
title: The Intern Who Only Approves Shipping
one_line_bet: Position Pocket Intern as a tiny anti-procrastination mascot: every sticky note either ships or gets judged.
why_it_might_impress: It connects the absurd toy action to a real desk behavior: stamping notes, closing loops, and making progress feel visible.
taste_priors:
  - "Weirdly useful" lands when the product changes a tiny habit.
  - The thread can be about a universal builder problem instead of only a novelty gag.
  - The phrase "only approves shipping" is memorable and product-specific.
hook: "I found the only intern who improves team velocity by doing almost nothing: it stamps 'ship it' on sticky notes."
execution_shape: 8 tweets: hook; problem of invisible progress; toy reveal; how the stamp becomes a ritual; three use cases; why absurd objects spread; soft CTA to gift one to the friend with 47 half-finished ideas.
risk: Needs crisp pacing so it does not become generic productivity advice.
feedback_question: Keep, revise, or reject the hook and argument?
```

## ConceptCard C: Performance Review For A Wind-Up Employee

```text
title: Performance Review For A Wind-Up Employee
one_line_bet: Write the thread as a deadpan performance review of Pocket Intern, giving the toy personality without copying a real creator voice.
why_it_might_impress: The format is inherently shareable because every tweet can be a joke and a feature reveal at once.
taste_priors:
  - Fake business artifacts fit the product's fake employee badge.
  - Deadpan review lets the thread show price, use case, and charm without sounding like ad copy.
  - People share characters more readily than product specs.
hook: "I conducted a 30-day performance review for our new intern. Strengths: stamps 'ship it.' Weaknesses: wind-up based compensation model."
execution_shape: 6-8 tweets: hook; role description; strengths; growth areas; desk impact; who should hire one; CTA framed as "open requisitions under $40."
risk: If too bit-heavy, the product benefit may disappear behind the joke.
feedback_question: Keep, revise, or reject the hook and argument?
```

## Recommendation

Pick ConceptCard B if the goal is the strongest commercial bridge between joke
and use. Pick ConceptCard C if the goal is maximum memorability. Pick
ConceptCard A if the goal is founder-Twitter immediacy.
