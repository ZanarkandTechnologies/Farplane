# One-Week Focused Mission (Historical Precedent)

## Use When

Use this precedent when a distant, high-volume ambition is making the user
avoid action. It informs the first proof phase of a feasible roadmap; it is not
the whole roadmap contract.

## Input Context

> I need to make $1 million by the end of the year, but the goal feels so hard
> that I keep avoiding it.

## Accepted Artifact

```text
Keep $1 million as the direction, but take it out of your active plan.

For the next seven days, your entire mission is one signed, paid $10,000
customer. Aim at that with full force. Do not plan the other $990,000 this week.

Right now, write the $10,000 result you can sell in one sentence, choose the
person most likely to need it, and contact them today.

One completed transaction is enough for this push. When the week ends, use
what actually happened to choose the next focused mission.
```

## Key Decisions

- Preserve the ambition as direction instead of arguing it down.
- Remove the distant total from the active horizon instead of decomposing it.
- Choose one meaningful completed result, not preparation for that result.
- Concentrate attention for one short push, then let evidence shape the next.
- End with a direct action toward the result.

## Acceptance Evidence

The operator rejected arithmetic decomposition, formal Feasibility Cards, and
multi-level planning. The accepted behavior was: keep the large goal, ignore
its remaining workload for now, and pursue one substantial weekly result with
full attention.

## Transferable Invariants

- Direction stays large; active attention becomes narrow.
- The focused mission completes a real loop and matters on its own.
- The response reduces cognitive load rather than representing the full plan.
- The immediate move directly advances the mission.
- The next mission is chosen after evidence, not forecast in advance.

## Incidental Details Not To Copy

- `$1 million`, `$10,000`, one customer, and seven days belong to this case.
- Other goals may use one publication, one real user, one delivery, or one
  honest capacity-bound week.
- “Full force” never overrides supplied safety, capacity, or external authority.

## Contrasting Failure

```text
Goal: Make $1 million.
Known / assumed: ...
Trust Ladder: first proof -> repeatable unit -> scale condition
Commitment: floor -> promise -> stretch
Portfolio placement: candidate
Do now: outline an offer for 25 minutes.
```

This fails because it turns the intimidating ambition into another dense
planning object and stops at preparation. The user needs one mission, not a
model of every future commitment.
