# Held-Out Forward Test

## Test Input

> I want to make five videos in two months, but the amount of work makes me
> freeze. I have no schedule or current portfolio data yet. Help me make it
> actionable right now.

## Resulting Feasibility Card

**Goal:** Make five videos in the next two months. The current blocker is the
volume of the whole goal, not proof that one video is impossible.

**Believable route:** Do not promise five equal projects now. Finish one
deliberately small video from idea through sharing, observe what it actually
takes, then use that evidence to decide whether and how to repeat it.

**Known / assumed:**

- Known: the desired outcome is five videos in two months; the unbounded amount
  of work causes freezing; there is no schedule, capacity estimate, or portfolio
  data.
- Assumed: a short video about something you can already explain can be made
  without new research or production equipment. The first proof tests that
  assumption and reveals the real friction.

**Trust Ladder:**

- **First proof:** Make one 45–90 second video about one idea you can explain
  from memory, then actually share it with one real person or in one existing
  channel. Done means it has a clear opening, one main point, an ending, is
  exported, and has been shared—not merely outlined or recorded.
- **Repeatable unit:** One similarly scoped video, made using the same
  idea-to-share loop and a recorded note of where the effort or resistance
  appeared.
- **Scale condition:** After the first proof, decide whether to commit to the
  second video using the actual effort, friction, and willingness to repeat the
  loop. Only after repeat evidence should the remaining count become a real
  commitment.

**Scope locks:**

- One idea, one viewer need, one main point; no research rabbit hole.
- Keep the first video to 45–90 seconds; use one recording setup and only edits
  needed to make it understandable.
- No new brand, publishing system, equipment, schedule, or batch plan is part
  of this proof.

**Commitment:**

- **Floor:** Complete and share one small video.
- **Promise:** Make no commitment for the other four until this proof supplies
  real effort data.
- **Stretch:** If the completed video feels repeatable, capture one sentence on
  what made it easier or harder and select the next similarly scoped idea.

**Do now:** Set a 25-minute timer. In a note, write three ideas you could explain
without research; circle the least intimidating one; record one rough 45-second
take that opens with the viewer problem and gives the one answer. Stop when the
timer ends—this starts the proof rather than requiring you to solve all five
videos today.

**Portfolio placement:** **candidate.** There is no supplied portfolio or
capacity context, so this goal is not being admitted, replaced, parked, or
written anywhere. It becomes a portfolio decision only after the first proof
and explicit portfolio context or write authority.

**Trust update:** Completing the proof shows whether one bounded video can move
from an idea to a shared artifact without becoming an undefined project. If it
does not complete, the stopping point identifies the constraint to reshape; it
does not prove that the five-video goal is impossible.

**One correction:** If you name the single audience these videos should help, the
first proof can be retargeted to one concrete viewer problem. If not, proceed
with the easiest idea you can explain from memory.
