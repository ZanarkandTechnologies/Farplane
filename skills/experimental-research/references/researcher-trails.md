# Researcher And Experiment Trails

Begin with multiple query formulations across the technical claim, task,
dataset, metric, and known baseline. For each useful paper, record version,
venue or archive, date, authors, affiliations, citations that shaped the method,
later work that tested it, and direct project/code/data links.

Follow researcher and lab trails when they reveal subsequent experiments,
negative results, implementation changes, talks, X discussions, or demos.
Follow repository contributors and issues when the executable method diverges
from the paper. Search independent replications and critiques before accepting
the authors' evaluation. YouTube talks and demonstrations can clarify setup and
lead to artifacts, but they do not replace a protocol or result table.

For X, inspect the relevant post, thread, replies, and linked work while
separating author promotion from independent reports. For YouTube, inspect the
video or transcript sections, chapters, description, and relevant comments.
Record actual, partial, denied, or unavailable access per modality; do not turn
a title, snippet, or metadata record into inspected content.

Maintain branch state as `open`, `corroborated`, `contradicted`, or `exhausted`.
Each material finding must update a hypothesis, trigger the next discriminating
query, or close the branch. When a trail is contradicted or blocked, backtrack
to the highest-value unresolved sibling. Stop only when remaining branches
cannot change the experiment ranking or the budget is exhausted, and report
what remains unsearched.

Normalize each result by task, dataset split, baseline, metric definition,
compute or hardware, sample count, uncertainty, and ablation coverage. Record
whether artifacts are available and maintained. When adoption depends on a
foreign vendor, proprietary device, or hosted API, compare lock-in, reproducibility,
cost, data handling, and a local fallback before ranking the experiment.
