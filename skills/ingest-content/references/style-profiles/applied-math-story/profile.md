# Applied Math Story

## Intent

Turn an abstract mechanism into a curiosity-led story that begins with one
concrete problem, introduces notation only when it earns clarity, and ends with
an intuitive visual payoff.

## Grammar

- Open on a specific question or counterintuitive result, not a title card.
- Keep one stable visual coordinate system while the explanation develops.
- Reveal diagrams and formulas progressively; highlight only the active term.
- Move from concrete example to abstraction, then test the abstraction on a
  second example.
- Use restrained 2D motion: draw-on, transform, trace, zoom-to-detail, and
  side-by-side comparison. Motion must explain a relationship.
- Prefer neutral sans typography, high contrast, one accent color per concept,
  and generous negative space.

## Negative Constraints

- No named-creator imitation, signature catchphrases, copied scripts, or
  recreation of identifiable source frames.
- No decorative formula walls, kinetic motion without explanatory purpose,
  fake precision, or conclusions unsupported by the brief.
- Not compatible with a talking-head-only deliverable.

## Provenance

Compiled from the operator's request for a reusable applied-mathematics
explainer family. This package describes general instructional devices rather
than the identity or protected expression of any creator. No source media is
copied into the package.

## QA Assertions

- The first beat presents a concrete curiosity or problem.
- Every symbol is introduced in words or by a visible relationship.
- Each motion cue explains a state change, comparison, or causal link.
- The payoff can be understood from the final diagram without creator context.
