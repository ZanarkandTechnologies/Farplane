# Lead Engine Review Rubric

## Provenance

Adapted from Greg Faxon's five lessons in his [$100M Leads summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-leads-summary). Applicability, grades, test design, and ranking rules are reviewer adaptations. Third-party book and research claims remain unverified.

The compact table indexes the criteria; the complete Before → Move → After/Result → Transfer trajectories follow below and are required evidence for every review.

Use this reference for every review. Grade each lesson:

- `0 unknown`: evidence is missing or contradictory.
- `1 absent/fragile`: the capability is absent or fails in ordinary use.
- `2 partial`: it exists but is inconsistent, unproven, or constrained.
- `3 effective`: repeated evidence shows it works without unacceptable harm.

Confidence is separate: `low | medium | high`.

| # | Criterion and diagnostic question | Weak Before | Stronger After | Source example, not prescription |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Valuable lead magnet:** Does free value create trust and a natural next step to the core offer? | Generic PDF, weak outcome, or disconnected giveaway. | Standalone value reveals a problem, provides a sample, or solves one step toward the paid outcome. | Package software, information, service, or product like a real offer with a clear CTA. |
| 2 | **Core Four mastery:** Is the business competently using the method appropriate to its stage before adding channels? | Sporadic tactics with no known inputs or output rates. | At least one suitable method is repeatable; expansion follows evidence and capacity. | Warm outreach → content → cold outreach → paid ads. |
| 3 | **More → Better → New:** Has the current method received enough volume and constraint improvement before expansion? | Channel hopping after a small or ambiguous sample. | Adequate repetitions establish a baseline; the largest funnel drop is improved before a new placement/platform/method. | Rule of 100, then improve the constraint, then expand. |
| 4 | **Customer-created growth:** Can satisfied customers create qualified new customers faster than churn removes them? | Referrals are accidental; product outcomes or asks are weak. | High-value customers get early wins and receive a clear, respectful introduction request. | Immediate three-way introduction after articulating mutual benefit. |
| 5 | **Level-appropriate scaling:** Is acquisition complexity appropriate to proven capability? | Multiple offers, avatars, platforms, hires, or spend before one method works. | Complexity increases only when inputs, economics, product value, and leadership are proven. | Progress from one offer/avatar/platform to multi-method departments. |

## Core Four Subrubric

| Method | Relationship / reach | Evidence to inspect | Typical fit boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm outreach | One-to-one / known people | Relevant network, asks, replies, qualified conversations, conversions | Common starting point; limited by network and founder time. |
| Free content | One-to-many / known audience | Topic selection, attention, retention, CTA, qualified inbound, assisted conversions | Useful when time and credible experience exceed cash. |
| Cold outreach | One-to-one / strangers | List quality, deliverability, relevance, reply, meeting, conversion, reputation cost | Useful when targeting is precise and follow-up capacity exists. |
| Paid ads | One-to-many / strangers | Gross profit, CAC, payback, creative, landing conversion, fulfillment capacity | Scale path only when economics and delivery can tolerate variance. |

Grade each method separately. An unused method is not automatically a problem;
first label it `required now | assessable | candidate later | not currently required`.
Grade only applicable or materially assessable methods. Lesson 2 evaluates
mastery and fit, not the number of active channels.

## Level Diagnostic

- **Level 1:** one offer, one avatar, one platform; network understands the offer.
- **Level 2:** the inputs and baseline for one method are known and personally repeatable.
- **Level 3:** trained execution can reproduce a method the founder understands.
- **Level 4:** product/referral behavior is strong; use 25% referral share only as an illustrative threshold, not a universal law.
- **Level 5:** at least two methods work across platforms with viable economics.
- **Level 6:** proven leaders own method/platform departments without founder dependence.

Do not promote a business by checklist count. Select the lowest missing
capability that blocks reliable progress.

## Criterion Card

Use the canonical [business review contract](../../../docs/contracts/business-strategy-review.md).
For this rubric, `consequence` must cover audience/customer and business consequences.

## Ranking Rule

Prefer the improvement closest to the observed bottleneck and business
objective. Require a believable path from activity to qualified demand, viable
economics, and deliverable customer value. Defer channel expansion when the
business lacks sufficient repetitions, a working message/offer, conversion
proof, referral-worthy outcomes, or fulfillment capacity.


## Application Trajectories


These are structured paraphrases of the source video. Each lesson contains
several moves because the video has five top-level branches rather than ten.
Use each trajectory as an analogy and record `adapt | reject | investigate`.

## 1. From disposable giveaway to offer-quality lead magnet

- **Before:** A generic free PDF or low-effort giveaway attracts attention but weakens trust and does not lead naturally to the paid offer.
- **Move:** Build free value with paid-product discipline: reveal a problem, give a representative sample/trial, or solve one meaningful step in a larger process. Package it with a real name, useful formats, clear CTA, and reason to act.
- **After/result:** The free asset demonstrates the standard of the paid work and makes the next step intelligible; it can be software, information, service, or physical product.
- **Source application:** Greg frames lead-magnet quality as a reputation test and recommends a paid next step on the thank-you page when the magnet is easier to advertise than the core offer.
- **Transfer test:** Does the free asset create a qualified bridge to the paid outcome, or just collect low-intent contacts?

## 2. From scattered acquisition to Core Four progression

### Warm outreach

- **Before:** An early business waits for inbound demand or pitches friends directly.
- **Move:** Work the known network with “Who do you know who…?”, cycle through it again, and treat early acquisition as substantial founder work.
- **After/result:** The first clients and real objections create market and offer evidence; Greg presents this as the normal starting point.

### Free content

- **Before:** Generic “how to” content competes broadly and produces little selection or retention.
- **Move:** Start in a narrow credibility pond, use first-person experience, improve topic/headline/native format, and sustain attention with lists, steps, stories, and value density.
- **After/result:** Useful content grows the warm audience, which strengthens later warm outreach and private asks.

### Cold outreach

- **Before:** A business sends sporadic messages to saturated lists and treats one pass as final.
- **Move:** Build a targeted list, personalize in proportion to volume/value, demonstrate value quickly, contact persistently across appropriate channels, and recycle as timing changes.
- **After/result:** The method becomes a measurable volume system instead of isolated persuasion attempts.

### Paid ads

- **Before:** Ads are treated as a shortcut before unit economics, proof, creative, or fulfillment capacity are known.
- **Move:** Start with audience/platform fit and economics, repel poor-fit viewers, focus on the first five seconds, reuse proven organic content/testimonials, and keep landing-page continuity.
- **After/result:** Losing tests are expected; winning ads can receive more spend when gross-profit:CAC and payback remain viable.

- **Transfer test:** Which one method fits the business's present relationships, evidence, time, money, and capacity—and what proof permits the next method?

## 3. From channel hopping to More → Better → New

- **Before:** A partially working method is abandoned for a shiny channel before it has a trustworthy sample or optimized constraint.
- **Move — More:** Apply enough comparable repetitions to establish a baseline; the source's “Rule of 100” is an example, not a universal quota.
- **Move — Better:** Find the funnel step with the largest meaningful drop and improve that constraint.
- **Move — New:** Expand only when marginal returns fall: new placement on the same platform, then a new platform for the same method, and only then a new Core Four method.
- **After/result:** Channel expansion becomes an evidence decision. Greg's resource branch favors content when time exceeds money, and cold/paid methods when money exceeds time.
- **Transfer test:** Is the current problem insufficient repetition, a known funnel constraint, or structural method mismatch?

## 4. From accidental referrals to customer-created growth

- **Before:** Referrals are treated as luck; weak product experience or failure to ask leaves growth dependent on acquisition spend.
- **Move:** Compare referral creation with churn, improve delivered value, focus on customers who benefit most, reduce overpromising, and force a meaningful early win in the first 48 hours where appropriate.
- **Ask:** Make referral value clear and request an immediate three-way introduction rather than a name to chase later.
- **After/result:** When referrals exceed churn, the customer base can grow without ads; when equal or lower, ads fund growth or merely replace losses.
- **Transfer test:** Is the business referral-ready because outcomes and trust are strong, and is the ask respectful and specific?

## 5. From premature complexity to level-appropriate scaling

- **Level 1 Before → After:** scattered offers/avatars/platforms → one offer, avatar, platform, with the network able to explain what is sold.
- **Level 2 Before → After:** inconsistent personal activity → known inputs and founder-repeatable execution for one method.
- **Level 3 Before → After:** founder bottleneck → trained people reproduce advertising the founder already understands profitably.
- **Level 4 Before → After:** acquisition masks product weakness → improve product and referral program until customer-created growth is material; the source uses 25% referral share illustratively.
- **Level 5 Before → After:** one fragile channel → at least two methods work across multiple platforms with viable economics.
- **Level 6 Before → After:** founder manages every method → proven leaders own method/platform departments.
- **Source caution:** Greg says progression is messy and requires patient tests of audiences, lead magnets, methods, and platforms.
- **Transfer test:** What is the lowest missing capability that blocks the next level, rather than which advanced tactic looks attractive?
