# Review Offer Rubric

## Provenance

Adapted from Greg Faxon's ten lessons in his [$100M Offers summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary). Criterion tests, grades, ethical gates, and ranking rules are reviewer adaptations, not verbatim creator claims. Third-party book and research claims remain unverified.

For every criterion, read its full Before → Move → After/Result → Transfer test in [application trajectories](application-trajectories.md). The compact table below is an index, not a substitute for that trace.

Grade every criterion:

- `0 unknown`: evidence is missing or contradictory.
- `1 absent/fragile`: the capability is absent or fails in ordinary use.
- `2 partial`: it exists but is inconsistent, unproven, or constrained.
- `3 effective`: repeated evidence shows it works without unacceptable harm.

Confidence is separate: `low | medium | high`.

| # | Criterion and diagnostic question | Weak Before | Stronger After | Source example, not prescription |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Category-of-one offer:** Why is this distinct rather than interchangeable? | Format-led commodity such as “weekly calls.” | Defined result, differentiated path, compelling value, credible risk reversal, and workable terms. | “20 clients in six months; continued work if missed.” |
| 2 | **Starving-market fit:** Does the market have urgent pain, purchasing power, reachability, and stable/growing demand? | Broad audience with vague desire or inability to pay. | A reachable buyer with costly pain and credible demand evidence. | Stadium crowd matters more than a better hot dog; unemployed job seekers may lack purchasing power. |
| 3 | **Specific niche and persistence:** Is person/problem/mechanism specific, and has it received a fair test? | Generic offer abandoned after one weak attempt. | Specific buyer, pain, and approach tested through a meaningful sample. | Shift-nurse weight loss versus generic weight loss. |
| 4 | **Expensive bargain:** Is price premium to substitutes but small versus credible value? | Discounting unchanged delivery to win deals. | Price and delivery support strong buyer outcomes and a defensible value-to-price gap. | Higher price can fund better service and buyer commitment. |
| 5 | **Value equation:** Are dream outcome and perceived likelihood high while delay and effort/sacrifice are low? | Big promise with a slow, uncertain, DIY journey. | Credible outcome, proof, fast first win, and major friction removed. | Sell the vacation, not the flight; done-for-you reduces effort. |
| 6 | **Visible value:** Can buyers and clients see progress and realized benefit? | Valuable work remains invisible. | Baseline, milestones, debriefs, and outcome recaps make value legible. | Train-arrival display, transparent vacuum canister, coaching debrief. |
| 7 | **Efficient obstacle coverage:** Is every material buyer-perceived blocker resolved using the lowest-cost reliable delivery form? | Predictable blockers are left to the customer or solved with unnecessary bespoke labor. | Each blocker maps to an adequate checklist, template, automation, partner, group, or bespoke intervention. | Shopping list/partner rather than personally shopping for each client. |
| 8 | **Honest scarcity and urgency:** What constraint or date remains true without marketing copy? | Resetting countdown or invented “limited spots.” | Real capacity, cohort date, waiting list, access rule, or announced price change is enforced. | Six real 1:1 client slots. |
| 9 | **Component and bonus architecture:** Does each named component remove an obstacle or accelerate success? | Vague bundle, clutter bonuses, or last-minute discount. | Core result is clear; components and bonuses make value specific without noise. | Toolbox decomposed into named tools; extra knives instead of discount. |
| 10 | **Fit-for-economics guarantee:** Does risk reversal address the buyer's main fear without breaking delivery economics? | Generic refund unrelated to effort, fulfillment, or buyer behavior. | Specific, memorable terms allocate risk credibly and may require success-producing actions. | Continued service if milestone is missed; plan-specific guarantee; reasoned anti-guarantee. |

## Ranking Rule

Prefer the improvement closest to observed buyer friction or the stated
objective. Market, delivered outcome, and proof constrain downstream work:
copy, bonuses, urgency, and guarantees cannot rescue an unwanted or ineffective
offer. Reject changes that depend on deception, unserviceable promises,
unaffordable risk, or added complexity without customer value.
