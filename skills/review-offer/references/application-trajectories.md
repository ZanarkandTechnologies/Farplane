# Offer Application Trajectories

These are structured paraphrases of the source video. Some are Greg's own
offer applications; others are book or market illustrations he used. The label
matters: never invent a personal result where the source only supplied an
example. For each criterion record `adapt | reject | investigate`.

## 1. From commodity coaching to a category-of-one promise

- **Before:** An offer is described by calls or general help and is easily price-compared.
- **Move:** Combine a defined result, differentiated package, premium price, credible guarantee, and favorable terms.
- **After/result:** The buyer evaluates a distinct result/path rather than interchangeable hours.
- **Greg's illustration:** A client-acquisition offer promising 20 clients in six months, with continued work if the milestone is missed.
- **Transfer test:** Can the promised result and risk reversal be delivered and measured for this buyer?

## 2. From an attractive idea to a starving market

- **Before:** The operator improves the product and persuasion while demand, purchasing power, reachability, or growth are weak.
- **Move:** Test the market on massive pain, purchasing power, targetability, and growth before polishing the offer.
- **After/result:** Offer work is concentrated where unmet need and ability to buy already exist.
- **Source illustrations:** A stadium's hungry crowd matters more than improving a hot dog; unemployed job seekers may have severe need but weak purchasing power; senior dating grows while newspapers shrink.
- **Transfer test:** Which market condition is weakest, and what observed evidence—not enthusiasm—supports it?

## 3. From generic positioning and market hopping to niche persistence

- **Before:** “Weight loss for everyone” competes as a commodity and is abandoned after one weak attempt.
- **Move:** Specify a person, problem, and counterintuitive mechanism that reverses a deep fear; give the offer a fair, measurable test before switching markets.
- **After/result:** The offer speaks to a recognizable situation and can command more when relevance and value are credible.
- **Source illustration:** Generic low-cost weight loss versus a premium shift-nurse program built around shift-timed constraints.
- **Transfer test:** Is weak performance structural market evidence, or merely an under-tested generic offer?

## 4. From discounting to an expensive bargain

- **Before:** Price is cut to win deals, reducing delivery resources, buyer commitment, perceived value, and seller conviction.
- **Move:** Price from credible customer value and add the delivery quality, proof, access, or implementation needed to widen the value-to-price gap.
- **After/result:** Higher price can fund better service and attract greater commitment, producing stronger results and proof—a virtuous cycle only if value is real.
- **Transfer test:** What conservative value does the buyer receive, and can delivery sustain the promise at the proposed price?

## 5. From a big claim and hard journey to a stronger value equation

- **Before:** The offer promises a distant outcome through a slow, uncertain, effort-heavy process.
- **Move:** Increase dream outcome and perceived likelihood while reducing delay and customer effort/sacrifice; prioritize an early emotional win.
- **After/result:** The buyer sees a more believable, faster, easier path without the result being weakened.
- **Source illustrations:** Sell the vacation rather than the flight; help a gym create an early $2,000 sale; done-for-you beats DIY when it removes real effort; Amazon wins through immediacy and ease.
- **Transfer test:** Which term—outcome, likelihood, delay, or effort—is the binding value constraint, and what can change without false promises?

## 6. From invisible work to perceived value

- **Before:** The service creates value, but buyers cannot see progress, saved effort, or completed work.
- **Move:** Surface baselines, milestones, time saved, decisions made, and Before/After outcomes during delivery.
- **After/result:** Identical or improved delivery becomes easier to understand, remember, and attribute.
- **Source illustrations:** Train-arrival maps improved satisfaction more than faster service; a transparent vacuum canister makes cleaning visible; Greg uses coaching debriefs to make client gains explicit.
- **Transfer test:** What existing value could become legible without manufacturing a vanity metric?

## 7. From one delivery method to efficient obstacle coverage

- **Before:** Predictable buyer blockers remain unsolved, or every blocker receives expensive bespoke labor.
- **Move:** List every perceived obstacle, then choose the lowest-cost reliable solution: checklist, template, automation, partner, group, or bespoke intervention.
- **After/result:** The offer feels complete while preserving delivery economics; scalable assets handle repeatable problems where adequate.
- **Source illustration:** A shopping list or delivery partner can solve food-shopping friction without the coach shopping personally for every client.
- **Transfer test:** Does the lighter solution actually resolve the blocker for the target customer?

## 8. From manufactured pressure to honest scarcity and urgency

- **Before:** Evergreen countdowns and vague “limited spots” reset, damaging trust.
- **Move:** State and enforce real capacity, cohort dates, waiting lists, access rules, enrollment windows, or scheduled price increases.
- **After/result:** Fence-sitters receive a truthful decision point and delivery stays within capacity.
- **Greg's application:** He cites a real capacity of six one-to-one clients and communicating a genuine annual price increase in advance.
- **Transfer test:** Would the constraint or date remain true if no marketing copy mentioned it?

## 9. From vague bundle or discount to component value

- **Before:** The offer appears as one undifferentiated bundle; sellers cut price when buyers hesitate.
- **Move:** Establish core value, then name components and bonuses that solve objections or accelerate success; favor tools/checklists when they reduce effort more than extra training.
- **After/result:** Buyers can see how the offer resolves their obstacles without learning that the price is negotiable.
- **Source illustrations:** A toolbox feels more concrete when its tools are named; classic knife offers reveal additional knives and a sharpener after the core value.
- **Transfer test:** Would removing this component make the offer less effective, or merely less busy?

## 10. From generic refunds to fit-for-economics risk reversal

- **Before:** A standard refund ignores fulfillment cost, customer action, misalignment risk, and the buyer's actual fear.
- **Move:** Identify the feared loss, then choose specific conditional, service/performance, unconditional, plan-specific, escape-hatch, or reasoned anti-guarantee terms.
- **After/result:** Risk is allocated credibly and the guarantee can reward behaviors that increase success without breaking the business.
- **Source illustrations:** Continue working until a milestone; refund fee plus ad spend when tightly controlled; “love it or leave it”; guarantee only an upfront plan.
- **Transfer test:** What risk can the business truly assume, measure, and honor—and what buyer behavior must be explicit?
