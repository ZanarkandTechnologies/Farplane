# Money Model Review Rubric

## Provenance

Adapted from Greg Faxon's ten lessons in his [$100M Money Models summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-money-models-summary). Criterion tests, the viability gate, grades, and ranking rules are reviewer adaptations. Third-party book and research claims remain unverified.

For every criterion, read its full Before → Move → After/Result → Transfer test in [application trajectories](application-trajectories.md). The compact table below is an index, not a substitute for that trace.

Use this reference for every review. Grade each criterion:

- `0 unknown`: evidence is missing or contradictory.
- `1 absent/fragile`: the capability is absent or fails in ordinary use.
- `2 partial`: it exists but is inconsistent, unproven, or constrained.
- `3 effective`: repeated evidence shows it works without unacceptable harm.

Confidence is separate: `low | medium | high`. A high-confidence grade of 1 is
more actionable than a low-confidence grade of 1.

| # | Criterion and diagnostic question | Weak Before | Stronger After | Source example, not prescription |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Offer sequence:** Is there a coherent path from first purchase through expansion, downsell, and continuity? | One isolated offer or unrelated products. | Each next offer solves a real adjacent customer problem. | Rental-car sequence: attraction → upsells → downsells → continuity. |
| 2 | **Easy first yes:** Can a qualified buyer start with proportionate risk and still receive standalone value? | Large commitment before trust or proof. | A valuable entry step reduces risk and reveals mutual fit. | $2,500 Strategy Sprint before a ~$30K annual engagement. |
| 3 | **Prescribed next solution:** Does delivery surface and justify the next problem to solve? | Generic upsell detached from diagnosis. | Evidence from the first engagement supports a personalized next step. | Strategy brief leads to implementation/accountability coaching. |
| 4 | **Value anchoring:** Does the buyer understand the full solution and tradeoff before evaluating the entry option? | Prices appear without a value frame or are manipulated with fake anchors. | A credible premium scope clarifies why a smaller start is lower risk. | Present annual engagement before the sprint. |
| 5 | **Rollover value:** Can prior spend or work reduce friction in the next purchase without hiding economics? | Starting over makes expansion feel duplicative. | Relevant prior payment/work visibly counts toward the larger solution. | Sprint payment becomes month one of annual coaching. |
| 6 | **Payment rail economics:** Are payment costs and alternatives transparent and customer-appropriate? | Hidden fees, one payment rail, or avoidable processing loss. | Transparent options balance buyer convenience, collection risk, and fees. | Card fee disclosed and waived for ACH. |
| 7 | **Payment-plan downsells:** Can payment timing change before the valuable scope is cut? | Immediate price discount or confusing menu of plans. | Sequential options preserve value while testing affordability/timing. | Pay in full → financing → split pay → three-pay → monthly. |
| 8 | **Scope downsells:** Can “no to this version” become a smaller honest solution? | Same offer repeatedly discounted. | Lower scope has explicit removed features and appropriate price. | Quarterly advisory with fewer calls and no between-call support. |
| 9 | **Upfront continuity:** Can ongoing value be committed and collected earlier without coercion? | Month-to-month is the only path or prepayment has no real value. | Prepayment has a fair incentive and clear delivery/cancellation terms. | Annual discount, bonus, or waived setup fee. |
| 10 | **Simple core:** Are multiple mechanisms supporting a proven core product rather than multiplying products? | Product sprawl fragments demand and delivery. | A small core captures most value; offers vary around it. | Sprint, annual, payment plans, and quarterly continuity around coaching. |

## Ranking Rule

Prefer an intervention when it is close to the stated objective, supported by
current evidence, valuable to the customer, economically meaningful, feasible
within capacity, and testable without masking other variables. Reject moves
that manufacture complexity, depend on deceptive anchoring, worsen delivery,
or merely shift cash timing while harming retention or trust.

For end-to-end calibration, read the [golden trace](golden-trace.md).
