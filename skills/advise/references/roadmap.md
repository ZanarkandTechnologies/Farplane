# Roadmap Mode

Load when `advise` runs with `mode: roadmap`: the question is how to get from
the current state to a goal, not which of several options to pick. Produce
enough phases to make progress legible, not a ticket program, staffing plan, or
precise forecast. Do not mutate tasks, calendars, or external systems.

## Sequencing rules

- **First proof is a completed loop.** Phase 1 ends in a paid commitment, an
  observed customer or user result, a shipped core workflow, or another loop
  that matters on its own. Strategy work, infrastructure, outlines, and
  polished portfolio artifacts are preparation, not proof.
- **Narrow the active horizon.** When a distant, high-volume goal is causing
  avoidance, keep it as the direction and take its remaining workload out of
  the active plan. Plan one focused mission; choose the next from what actually
  happened, not from a forecast.
- **Exit on observation.** Each phase has an observable exit milestone and the
  dependency that makes the next phase feasible. Use ranges or conditions when
  evidence does not support dates.
- **Only sequence-changing risks.** Name a risk only when it can alter
  sequence, scope, or viability, with its cheapest evidence check.
- **Ask at most one question**, and only when its answer changes the sequence;
  otherwise give a provisional path with assumptions marked.

## Output

```text
Goal and horizon
Current state and constraints (observed vs assumed)

Phase 1 — <purpose>
  Exit milestone: <observable completed loop>
  Main risk / evidence check: <risk -> cheapest check>
Phase 2 — ...   (three to five phases; fewer when the next mission is the plan)

Assumptions that could change the roadmap
Immediate next move (advances Phase 1 directly)
```

## Precedent: one focused mission

Accepted by the operator (synthetic, sanitized). Input: "I need to make $1
million by the end of the year, but the goal feels so hard that I keep
avoiding it."

```text
Keep $1 million as the direction, but take it out of your active plan.
For the next seven days, your entire mission is one signed, paid $10,000
customer. Do not plan the other $990,000 this week.
Right now, write the $10,000 result you can sell in one sentence, choose the
person most likely to need it, and contact them today.
When the week ends, use what actually happened to choose the next mission.
```

Rejected: arithmetic decomposition, feasibility cards, a commitment ladder,
and "outline an offer for 25 minutes" — another dense planning object that
stops at preparation. The amounts, one customer, and seven days belong to this
case; other goals may use one publication, one real user, or one delivery.
