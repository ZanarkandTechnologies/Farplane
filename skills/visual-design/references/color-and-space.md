# Colour and Space

Use this when defining the colour roles and spacing rhythm of a visual system,
and when saved taste (a Tasty Pack) says "empty space", "beige", or a palette.

## Three-colour build

```text
palette = ground (~60%) -> figure (~30%) -> accent (~10%)
works(ground + figure) is required before the accent is added
```

1. **Ground**: the page or frame field. It sets the mood. A warm neutral
   (oat, greige) makes dark inks look printed and accents look deliberate.
2. **Figure**: type and the main shapes. Body text needs 4.5:1 against the
   ground; tints of the figure are allowed.
3. **Accent**: one loud hue on the single thing the eye must find (primary
   CTA, key word, cursor, live state). If it appears in more than one role, it
   is no longer an accent.

Check the render at each step: with ground and figure only, the page must
already read. A fourth hue needs a data or state role (see
`brand-product-register.md`), never decoration.

Worked example (Alena, @createwithalena, saved 13 Oct 2026): oat `#eceadb`
ground, blue `#224d95` figure, hot pink `#d93c80` accent; marine `#2b3f73`
ground, red `#d82d32` figure, butter `#feeea6` accent.

## Space budget

"Lots of whitespace" is not a spec. Set the budget in numbers:

| Measure | Brand register | Product register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject or content footprint in the first view | 10-35% of the area | set by the task, not a budget |
| Maximum text measure | 45-65 characters | 60-90 characters |
| Content column | at most 60% of a desktop viewport | full width when the workflow needs it |
| Section spacing | at least 2x the largest inner gap | consistent grid gap |

The ground carries no colour and no containers; colour lives in the objects
placed on it (props, imagery, the accent). Empty space in the product register
means fewer layers, not a sparser task.

Worked example (Muse Wonder launch film, Meta, saved 13 Oct 2026): every
vignette sits on a seamless greige cyc (wall `#7a7772`-`#8a877e`, floor
`#abaca3`-`#b3b9af`) taking roughly 10-25% of the frame; only props carry
saturation; one blue is saved for the caption cursor and end card.

## Saved taste versus standing rules

- Tasty Pack elements arrive with recipes that hold exact values. Copy the
  values into the steal table; do not paraphrase them into adjectives.
- Standing operator rules beat a saved reference: banned hues, a product UI's
  neutral rule, or a stated palette. Reject the clashing element in the
  receipt, name the rule, and offer a substitute from the same pack.
- Borrow the pattern, not the marks: a source's brand colour, wordmark, or
  people are never the new brand's.
