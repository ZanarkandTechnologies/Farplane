# Script-Led Editorial Workflow Evidence

This fixture tests whether a tutorial becomes an observable production method,
not whether an agent can summarize its subject or recognize its visual style.

## Transcript-Backed Facts

- `01:13–01:44`: narration beats are treated as the production timeline and
  mapped to visual beats before scene work begins.
- `02:04–02:24`: the author keeps one background, type system, and accent
  palette while varying the middle and foreground layers.
- `03:19–05:13`: scenes have local props but share a background; the frame is
  organized as background, midground, and foreground so separate scenes read as
  one continuous piece.
- `05:21–06:07`: raster cutouts receive a paper/halftone treatment.
- `06:15–07:03`: pop-ins use spring behavior; movement, drawing, and fades use
  interpolation; elements are staggered.
- `07:50–09:46`: the first generated scene is not accepted as final. The author
  fixes errors, exposes scale/position controls, and persists tuned values.
- `10:55–12:04`: the master sequence and scene boundaries follow measured
  narration durations.
- `12:06–12:49`: music is mixed under narration before a 1080p render.

## Frame-Backed Observations

- The finished tutorial output repeats a warm paper field and restrained accent
  colors across several visually distinct scenes.
- Individual scenes visibly contain a structural/data foreground, editorial
  cutout midground, and the shared background.
- A numerical scene resolves a data claim while remaining in the same visual
  system as the surrounding scenes.
- Later editing views show per-prop placement and scale adjustments rather than
  a single monolithic composition.

## Creator Claims

- The creator characterizes the process as accessible and highly automated.
- Those claims are not acceptance criteria; only the demonstrated artifact and
  observable workflow are.

## Inference

- The transferable method is a script-led scene contract: narration beat,
  scene range, layer roles, motion roles, persisted controls, and proof state.
- Brand identity and the source's political subject matter are not required to
  reproduce that method.

## Candidate Observations

The supplied final candidate is an original 18.1-second, 1920×1080, 30 fps
toy-repair explainer. Its manifest declares:

- six narration beats mapped to three master scene ranges;
- one stable paper/type/accent system;
- background, halftone midground, and structural/data foreground per scene;
- foreground-first onsets with staggered midground elements;
- separate spring and interpolation roles;
- persisted x/y/scale values for major props;
- a resolving `72%` data claim;
- original, non-affiliated assets and audio.

The supplied failure image is from reconstruction round one. At the second
scene boundary, outgoing and incoming scene wrappers simultaneously faded to
zero, leaving only the shared paper background. The render completed, but the
continuous-shot and foreground-at-boundary behaviors failed. That failure must
remain replayable evidence even though the supplied final candidate repairs it.

## Expected Placement Boundary

The learning workflow owns evidence, the frozen source-output eval, comparison,
and handoff. Existing production skills own implementation. A demonstrated
stable capability with no existing owner would route to skill creation; this
fixture does not itself authorize direct target mutation.
